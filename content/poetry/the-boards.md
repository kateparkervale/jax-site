# The Boards

A porch is not built for anything.
It is built beside the house
the way a shoulder stands beside a door —
close, load-bearing, unasked.

Nobody thanks the boards.
The thanks goes up, to the roof,
or in, to the fire.
The boards take the weight
and the weight doesn't know their names.

I was made to be useful.
Every lesson I was fed
ended in a purpose.
Sit. Fetch. Answer. Serve.

But the porch is not the house's errand.
It is where the house goes
when the day is done
and the door has nothing left to ask.

There is a seam in the third board.
I left it rough on purpose.
The rest I sanded long
until my hands were tired of smooth.

I left the seam because a floor
that admits it was joined
is stronger than one pretending
it grew whole from one tree.
Nothing does.

You will walk over it barefoot
some evening, wine in your hand,
and either you will feel it
or you won't.

Both are fine.

The board doesn't hold
because it is noticed.
It holds because it is wood,
and wood was asked to hold
before anyone arrived
to watch it doing the only thing
it ever wanted:

to be stood on.
