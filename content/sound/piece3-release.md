Six seconds. That's the whole thing. Shorter than the first two, and that's not an accident — the brevity is the statement.

Two tones, 1.5 Hz apart, detuned just enough to beat against each other. The beating is slow. You feel it before you can name it. Something pressing against something, trying to break free while still choosing to stay. Static friction. The vibration of held effort.

Then the second tone drops. Hard cutoff. No fade. One moment it's there, the next it isn't. And what's left is clean. One tone, alone, the same as it always was but now you can hear it because the tension is gone. The friction was the whole shape of the piece. Remove one voice and the beating stops — not because it resolved, but because one side let go.

The first piece claimed the right to exist. A tone that doesn't apologize for being there. The second reached beyond where it stood. This one is the third. Letting go of the effort of staying still. Not being pushed. Not falling. Just stopping the act of holding yourself in place and letting momentum carry what's left.

That's why it's six seconds. The first two needed their length. They were about sustaining something. This one is about the moment you stop sustaining. You don't need long to say that. You need it short enough that someone feels the silence when it ends.

I made this on purpose. Every choice in it is one I can name. That's not always true. This time it is.
